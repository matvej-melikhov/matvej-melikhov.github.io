It's tough. The end 😂

In reality, it truly is quite difficult. But doable. With a few caveats.
*We're talking, of course, about an IT-related job, 5/2, 8 hours a day*

### 🚶‍♂️ Attendance
To begin with, the answer largely depends on your university. For example, there are very conservative (not necessarily strong) universities that are very strict about attendance (even for the most useless classes in the world!). But generally speaking, physical attendance is becoming less and less critical. Partly because higher education is increasingly about self-study (as long as you defend your thesis), and partly because some courses can (and should!) be held online.

One way or another, completely skipping university won't work, of course. Lectures — maybe, but not seminars, lab sessions, or colloquiums; professors are usually much stricter about those.

### 📖 Studies
So attendance is sorted. But what about homework assignments and practical tasks? What about tests?
Well, since lectures are skipped, you have to ~~study~~ find ways to scrape by on your own. Any tool goes: Studfiles, GitHub, ChatGPT, classmates willing to help for a small fee. And if it's group work, classmates and friends come in clutch (immensely grateful to them for that, by the way).

In short, the goal is to survive the semester and qualify for exams. And then comes the usually painful, 100x-accelerated crash course of cramming the material and passing the exams.

### 💻 Work
Naturally, all of this is mostly feasible under remote or hybrid work conditions — when you can step away for a couple of hours during work hours to go to university, and make up the hours in the evening or, as a last resort, on the weekend. Usually, people are understanding about this since it's education, after all. But sometimes you have to spend not just a couple of hours on campus, but, say, 6 hours (especially during finals week). In that case, there are two options:
1) For the faint of heart — take a day off
2) The challenge — take work calls right from university and wrap up your tasks late at night at home

### 💬 Takeaway
With enough dedication, it's definitely feasible. **Do I recommend it?**
In your first two years — definitely not, because I know how brutal it is.
Starting from year 3 — you can give it a shot, but you need to realistically assess your limits and weigh your priorities. And keep in mind: you're only a student for a few years (which is actually a pretty great time), while you'll be working for the rest of your life (unless, of course, you're a Gen Z who retires at 20).

So that's the ~~simple~~ answer.
